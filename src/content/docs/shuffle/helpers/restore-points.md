A **restore point** is a saved copy of the system, so the computer can go back to how it was. The computer takes one before each update, before a Helper frees up space, installs or removes an app, and at other moments such as when it starts. It clears old ones by itself.

Your own files are not part of a restore point, so going back does not undo your work.

## See them

Press <kbd><a href="/start/first-steps/meta-key/">Meta</a></kbd> to open Search, type **Restore points**, and tap it. Each restore point shows when it was taken and why, such as **Before updating the computer**. **Show more** lists older ones.

## Go back to one

1. Tap the restore point you want.
2. The Helper lists the steps. Tap **Restart now**.
3. In the startup menu, open **Snapshots**, then pick the one the Helper named.
4. Look around. Nothing is kept until you say so: restarting normally comes back to now.
5. If it is right, open **Restore points** again and tap **Keep this**. If not, tap **Restart normally**.

If the computer's startup menu does not list restore points, the Helper says so, because there is no safe way back from there.

To undo one recent change instead of going back a whole restore point, use [What changed](/shuffle/helpers/undo-a-change/).

## Clear old ones

A button such as **Clear 4 older ones** deletes the older restore points to free space. To delete one, choose it and tap **Delete**. The Helper always asks first, and asks for your password in the system's prompt.
